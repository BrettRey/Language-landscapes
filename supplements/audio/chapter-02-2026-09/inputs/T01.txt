For most people, the word “standard” has positive connotations. Consider the words that we use together with it: pairs like “gold standard” and “quality standard,” or “standard of living” and “standard of care.” If you have high standards, or exceed them, that's a good thing. We set educational standards, environmental standards, and technology standards, all of which are supposed to make our education, our environment, and our technology better.

Standards, in short, are things we aspire to. And Standard English, with a capital S, may sound like something to aim for. You might think it's equivalent to good English, or grammatical English, or literary English. But it's not that simple, not that clear, and not that straightforward to define or to aim for.
