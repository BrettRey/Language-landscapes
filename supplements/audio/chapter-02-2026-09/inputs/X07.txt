So it seems that our processing ability may help us decide what is grammatical and what is not.

With this in mind, here is example six, which carries no judgment mark.

Example six. “After the trip, my suitcase sat unpacked on the floor for days.”

The chapter shows two photographs side by side and asks which one the sentence describes. On the left is a suitcase with clothes in it. On the right is a suitcase that is empty.

Many people say the sentence describes the first suitcase, the one on the left with the clothes in it, but that is incorrect. The first suitcase is packed. You put clothes in when you pack a suitcase. The second one has been unpacked. All the clothes have been removed.

Why would anybody get this wrong? The people who have thought about this question often feel that there's something tricky in the negation, the “un” at the beginning of “unpacked.” After all, if you replaced it with “packed,” the meaning would be perfectly clear. So is there some kind of glitch in the way we process language that messes with our comprehension sometimes? Maybe we can't rely on instinct to judge what is grammatical at all.
