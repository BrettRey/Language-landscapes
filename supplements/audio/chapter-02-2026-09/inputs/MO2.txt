Or consider the following conversation, recorded by Nishimura. Some of the speaker's words are Japanese, and I'll give the translation after each one.

B.C. ni iku toki, meaning “when I went to B.C.”, hikooki de yomoo to omotte kara, meaning “thinking I would read it on the plane”, I bought it, eh? So, it's not finished yet. She laughs. And it's hard, 'cause me-nanka, meaning “a person like me”, moo, meaning “really”, hon-nanka yomu to, meaning “when I read a book”, cover-to-cover yomanakattara, meaning “unless I read it cover-to-cover”, if I stop dokka de, meaning “at some point”, I forget the story.

This kind of code-switching is perfectly grammatical for any English-Japanese bilingual, but much of it seems ungrammatical for other English speakers.
