Exercises. There are ten. Pause after each one if you want to work it through.

One. Explain the concept of “standard” in the context of language. How is it different from the idea of “good” or “correct”?

Two. Why is the distinction between Standard English and Standard Englishes, with an “es” on the end, important?

Three. Provide an example of a shibboleth and explain how it functions as a social marker.

Four. What are some limitations of relying solely on intuition to determine grammaticality?

Five. The text argues that meaning is neither a necessary nor a sufficient condition for grammaticality. Explain this claim with an example.

Six. What factors contribute to a form-and-meaning pairing being considered grammatical for a specific group of speakers in a particular situation?

Seven. Describe a situation where an utterance might be considered ungrammatical even if it successfully conveys meaning.

Eight. How can the concept of audience inform our understanding of grammaticality?

Nine. What is meant by the term “normative” in the context of language?

And ten. What is the role of a TESL teacher in navigating the complexities of Standard English and grammaticality for their students?
