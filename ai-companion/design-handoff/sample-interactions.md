## Example 1

### User question

What distinction does the book make between categories and functions?

### Paraphrased answer shape

The book treats categories and functions as different kinds of grammatical description. Categories are what units are, like noun, verb, adjective, noun phrase, or clause. Functions are the jobs those units do in structure, like subject, object, head, modifier, or determiner. One category can serve different functions in different contexts, and the same function can be filled by different categories.

## Example 3

### User question

Explain grammaticality in plain language.

### Paraphrased answer shape

The book treats grammaticality as more than “does this follow a rule.” It is about whether a form fits the accepted form-meaning pairings of a speech community. That means syntax matters, but so do meaning, discourse, usage, and community norms. Some strings can sound wrong in one context and acceptable in another.

### Citation line

Source: The Nature of Grammaticality > Form-meaning pairings
