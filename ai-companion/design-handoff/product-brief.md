# Product Brief

## Product

An AI companion for *Language landscapes*, a full-length book by Brett Reynolds about English grammar, syntax, phonology, vocabulary, writing systems, pragmatics, discourse, conversation, and language teaching.

The product should let readers:

- ask questions about the book
- get grounded answers tied to specific chapters and sections
- inspect the source passages behind those answers
- browse the book if they do not know what to ask yet

## Current source material

- 24 chapter-level files in the current export
- 648 retrieval chunks
- about 121,117 clean words
- corpus lives in `book_chunks.jsonl`

## Primary audience

- language teachers
- TESL / applied linguistics students
- curious readers who want guided access to the book

## Secondary audience

- reviewers or editors sampling the book quickly
- instructors deciding whether to assign it
- readers who want help locating discussions across chapters

## Core product promise

This is not “an AI that knows grammar.”

It is “a guided interface to this specific book.”

The value comes from:

- grounded answers
- clear citations
- easier discovery of relevant chapters
- faithful explanation of the book’s distinctions

## User problems to solve

1. Readers do not know where in the book a topic is covered.
2. Readers want a quick explanation without losing the path back to the source.
3. Readers need confidence that the answer came from the book, not a model’s generic prior knowledge.
4. New visitors may not know what kinds of questions this book can answer.

## Scope for the mock-up

In scope:

- public landing surface
- conversational question interface
- chapter browser
- cited answer state
- retrieved source passage inspection
- mobile adaptation

Out of scope:

- payments
- user accounts
- collaborative workspaces
- analytics dashboards
- CMS/admin tooling
- full audiobook player integration

## Tone

- serious but not stiff
- teacher-facing and curious
- intellectually trustworthy
- human rather than corporate

## Desired emotional effect

The product should feel like:

- entering a carefully annotated study
- being guided by a thoughtful teacher
- having a map of the book rather than being dropped into a search box

It should not feel like:

- a random AI wrapper around a PDF
- a startup support bot
- a generic chat app with book branding pasted on top
