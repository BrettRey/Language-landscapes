# Cross-pollinate run, 2026-06-21

**Project:** *Language Landscapes* (forthcoming, Language Science Press, TBLS)
**Sampled:** 10 papers from `literature/`, after excluding ~56 already-cited author tokens (pool 634)
**Accepted:** 5 (2 divergent, 3 convergent)

The run was anchored on the live frontier: the multilingual localization effort (Japanese, Thai, Tagalog, Indonesian, Bengali/Hindi, Korean editions) and its two hard open questions, carry-over vs localized scope and terminology transfer (keep English metalanguage vs coin local equivalents while preserving the category/function and determinative/determiner distinctions that traditional local grammars collapse). Four of the five accepted insights bear directly on localization; the philosophy-of-truth and epistemic-injustice ones are the most useful and the most surprising.

## Divergent insights (challenge framing, suggest new directions)

### 1. Localization is metalinguistic enfranchisement, not translation fidelity
**Paper:** `literature/fricker_2007_epistemic_injustice.md`
**Scores:** novelty 9, coherence 9, usefulness 9, disruption 8 (avg 9.0)

Fricker separates *testimonial* injustice (a speaker's credibility is deflated by prejudice) from *hermeneutical* injustice (the community simply lacks the concepts to make sense of an experience). Teachers in the localization target contexts face a structural version of the second: when a language's pedagogical-grammar tradition collapses category and function, or has no term separating determinative from determiner, teachers cannot articulate structural facts about English that they can perceive. That reframes the keep-English-metalanguage-vs-coin-local-equivalents question. It is not a fidelity trade-off; coining or borrowing terms is hermeneutical resource-expansion, and collapsing the distinction in translation is a form of hermeneutical impoverishment for the teaching community.

**Suggested action:** Use the hermeneutical-injustice frame in the localization/translator guidance (and possibly a sentence in the categories-and-functions chapter): preserving the category/function distinction matters because it gives teachers the interpretive resources to say what they already half-see. This converts open question (2) from a pedagogical preference into an ethical-epistemic argument that the localizers can stand behind.
**Critic's note:** Reframes localization from translation fidelity to metalinguistic enfranchisement; directly addresses the status of coining new category terms in Japanese/Thai/Bangla; highly novel framing for the pedagogy gap.

### 2. Getting CGEL terms to "take" is an enregisterment problem
**Paper:** `literature/agha2005-voice-footing-enregisterment.md`
**Scores:** novelty 8, coherence 8, usefulness 8, disruption 7 (avg 8.0)

Agha's enregisterment model (a register's repertoire, social range, and social domain are maintained or transformed through speech chains that carry metapragmatic stereotypes) describes exactly the mechanism by which a translated metalanguage either spreads or gets swallowed. The localization risk Stefan's reviewers already flagged (traditional local terms reasserting themselves) is an enregisterment risk: will CGEL's category/function vocabulary become enregistered in Japanese or Thai teacher education, or will the locally enregistered traditional terms overwhelm it? Agha's *appropriate* vs *tropic* use maps onto whether a translated "determinative" is read as the CGEL concept or as the nearest familiar school-grammar term.

**Suggested action:** Give localizers a metapragmatic-scaffolding brief, not just a glossary: each load-bearing term needs teacher notes and paired congruent/noncongruent examples that actively block the gravitational pull of the traditional term. Frames the localized glossary as register-building work, which is also a concrete answer to "what exactly do I hand each localizer?"
**Critic's note:** Enregisterment models the localization risk directly; metapragmatic scaffolding is a concrete implementation lever.

## Convergent insights (reinforce or extend existing direction)

### 3. Truth pluralism's one-concept/many-realizers structure for the translator's guide
**Paper:** `literature/iep_pluralist_theories_of_truth.md`
**Scores:** novelty 8, coherence 8, usefulness 9, disruption 3 (avg 8.3)

Truth pluralism (Lynch's manifestation functionalism, Wright's one-concept-many-properties) faces the same shape of problem as the localization effort: a single superordinate concept realized by different local properties, with the standing threat that a local realizer gets mistaken for the concept itself. It supplies clean vocabulary for why "determinative" (category) and "determiner" (function) survive translation even into a language with no existing equivalent word: the concept is fixed by its distributional diagnostics, and the realizer may vary or be absent. The "problem of domain individuation" maps onto deciding which local pedagogical term, if any, realizes the CGEL category.

**Suggested action:** Open the translator's guide with the one-concept/many-realizers frame stated plainly (no need to cite Lynch/Wright unless the localizer is a philosopher): the concept is fixed by what you *test* for, local words may differ or be missing, and the job is to preserve the diagnostics rather than to find one perfect equivalent word. This is the cleanest available articulation of the terminology-transfer policy.
**Critic's note:** Directly addresses the live localization problem; supplies philosopher-grade vocabulary for the translator's guide.

### 4. Convergent evolution as the biological precedent for projectible categories
**Paper:** `literature/powell2020neuroanatomy.md`
**Scores:** novelty 7, coherence 8, usefulness 7, disruption 5 (avg 7.3)

Powell argues centralized nervous systems arose independently several times because the functional pressures are robust enough to channel evolution toward similar solutions from different starting points. That is the paradigm case of HPC-style robust replicability, a cluster of co-occurring properties held together by underlying constraint, so convergence is expected rather than coincidental. The octopus case (a clearly independent origin reaching high cognition) is a vivid analogy for why a localized edition built from a different grammatical tradition might still converge on the same category distinctions.

**Suggested action:** In the grammaticality chapter (Ch 20) or wherever the HPC/naturalist framing is introduced, use the convergence argument to motivate categories as projectible clusters that track real clustering under constraint, not arbitrary labels. Doubles as reassurance to localizers: independent traditions converging on the same joints is the expected outcome, not a translation artifact. (Note: Powell is already adjacent to Brett's portfolio via the *Contingency and Convergence* thread; this is a textbook-facing use of it.)
**Critic's note:** Biological precedent for HPC-style robustness; the octopus case is an apt analogy for independent traditions converging.

### 5. Zipfian skew as empirical backing for the naturalist observation stance
**Paper:** `literature/wolters2024word-referent.md`
**Scores:** novelty 6, coherence 8, usefulness 7, disruption 2 (avg 7.0)

Wolters et al. show that frequency skew (Zipfian distribution) is not noise to filter out but the very structure that makes word-referent learning possible: predictability from high-frequency items bootstraps the low-frequency ones. That is HPC projectibility instantiated at the level of lexical acquisition, and it gives the naturalist "watch the distribution" stance a cognitive, not merely philosophical, justification.

**Suggested action:** Cite Wolters et al. in the vocabulary chapter (Ch 8) bridging paragraph the reviewer asked for, to argue that attending to how often items occur, and in what contexts, is cognitively productive work rather than rule-avoidance. A concrete patch for the flagged "mechanics of application thin" learning goal in Ch 8.
**Critic's note:** Citation-ready for a Ch 8 bridging paragraph; defends frequency-attentiveness as pedagogically productive.
