Hi, Murat

Three good questions, and the right ones to ask before committing.

(a) Why translate, if a teacher can read English? Because reading dense English and having the concepts ready to hand are not the same thing. A Turkish teacher can follow the book in English and still have no Turkish vocabulary for the distinctions it draws, which means they can't easily explain them to a colleague or a class. The translation gives the ideas a home in Turkish so they can travel.

(b) "Load-bearing terms" means the small set the analysis leans on: category vs function, determinative vs determiner, count vs non-count, a handful more. The aim is to preserve each distinction, the test that tells you when the term applies. Coining a fully natural Turkish word for each one is not the point. Where Turkish has no word that draws the same line, say so plainly and keep the English term, rather than reach for a traditional term that quietly merges two things the book keeps apart. That's much less work than translating every analytic flourish, and it's the part only you can do.

(c) I don't have a German or French sample to show you the format, I'm afraid. But the principle above is the format: faithful where Turkish cooperates, explicit where it doesn't. I'll send the master term list so you can see exactly which distinctions are in play, and you can judge the real size of the job from there.

And don't let the harder passages put you off. The parts that matter for the translation are these distinctions, not every argument in the book. Your read on the market is right too: an academic audience is a good audience, and slow is fine. There's no deadline.
