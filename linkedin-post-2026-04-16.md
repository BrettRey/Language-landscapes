My textbook, Language Landscapes: The ESL Teacher's Guide to How English Works, is coming soon from Language Science Press. The catalogue page just went live.

I wrote this book because most grammar references for ESL teachers either oversimplify English into rules that don't hold up in real classrooms, or assume a level of linguistic training that working teachers don't have time to acquire. Language Landscapes tries to occupy the middle ground: rigorous enough to be trustworthy, practical enough to change how you think about the language you're teaching.

It covers phonology (including connected speech), vocabulary, orthography, syntax, information structure, coherence, pragmatics, and fluency across skills. The analytical framework draws on the Cambridge Grammar of the English Language (CGEL), but everything is written for teachers, not linguists.

The book will be open access (CC BY 4.0) -- free to read, share, and adapt.
